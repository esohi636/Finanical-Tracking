Inside the duration calculation

Hypothetical Treasury · $1,000 face · 4% fixed coupon · 10 years · Semiannual payments
P = Σ CFₜ / (1 + y/2)ᵗ
DMac = Σ [(t/2) × PV(CFₜ)] / P
Dmod = DMac / (1 + y/2)
DV01 ≈ P × Dmod × 0.0001

Sources & methodology

What each series measures
  • DGS10 — 10-year Treasury constant-maturity yield, Board of Governors via FRED. Used as a yield-to-maturity proxy to discount the hypothetical bond’s cash flows.
  • THREEFYTP10 — term premium on a 10-year zero-coupon bond, Board of Governors via FRED. This is the Kim–Wright model estimate, not the New York Fed’s ACM series. The assignment permits any public data. Term premium is estimated and can be revised.
  • MORTGAGE30US — average U.S. 30-year fixed mortgage rate, Freddie Mac’s Primary Mortgage Market Survey via FRED. This is a borrowing rate, not a mortgage-backed security price or an individual loan offer.
Alignment, historical coverage & limitations

The embedded snapshot covers January 2018 through December 2024. It contains the last published mortgage observation in each calendar month, rather than monthly averages. Treasury yield and term premium are matched to the latest available nonmissing daily observation on or before that mortgage observation date. The exported CSV includes the underlying Treasury observation dates.

Weekly mortgage observations summarize a survey period, while the Treasury series are daily observations. Matching dates does not make the underlying measurement periods identical. Freddie Mac changed its survey methodology on November 17, 2022; comparisons crossing that date require care.

The bond is reset to a full 10-year maturity at every observation, with the same 4% coupon. This makes historical duration comparable across dates; it does not track a single aging Treasury security. The calculation assumes a flat discount rate equal to DGS10, immediate coupon-date settlement, no accrued interest, and no transaction costs. It is a transparent illustrative measure, not a quoted duration for a specific Treasury CUSIP.

Mortgage rates do not equal Treasury yields plus term premium: term premium is already part of long-term Treasury yields. A common movement does not establish that term premium caused the mortgage-rate change.

Reproduce the data

Download the public histories from DGS10, THREEFYTP10, and MORTGAGE30US. Select the final mortgage observation each month and match the other series backward by date. Export data above downloads all 84 aligned observations and the calculated bond metrics.
